Pyramid

21 June 2026 · 1 min read

I have sat in on meetings between my dad and his CA and watched the whole thing. The CA comes in, reviews the books, talks through taxes, flags a few things, charges a fee, leaves.

And every time I sat there thinking, even I could do most of this with AI.

Not because CAs are bad at their jobs. But because what they do, pulling numbers, structuring reports, flagging compliance issues, giving financial advice based on existing data, is exactly what models like Claude and ChatGPT are already doing better and faster.

My research paper asks whether AI is making business consultants obsolete or creating a fundamentally new kind of consulting. The more I read, the more I think the answer is both, depending on which part of the pyramid you sit in.

The bottom of the pyramid, the structured, repeatable, data-driven work, is already being replaced. The Dell’Acqua and BCG study showed this clearly. AI made average consultants perform like experts on routine tasks.

But the top is different. Judgment, relationships, knowing when the numbers are lying. That part nobody has replaced yet, and I am not convinced anyone will soon.

The pyramid is not falling. It is just getting much shorter at the bottom.
