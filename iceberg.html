Iceberg

30 November 2025 · 1 min read

Most people think AI is just prompting. Type something in, get something out. Done. I genuinely wish it was that simple.

I have spent months inside it. Not casually. Obsessively. Building businesses, automating my dad’s operations, trying to solve problems that people dedicate entire careers to. And I still won’t say I know it all.

What nobody tells you is that AI does not think for you. It responds to you. And if you do not know what you are asking, you get nothing useful back.

Every word in a prompt is a decision. The framing, the context, the constraints, the tone, all of it changes the output completely. I have rewritten the same prompt forty times to get one paragraph right, or one piece of code working. I have seen the difference between someone who uses AI and someone who actually understands it, and that gap is bigger than most people want to admit.

I have done things in ten minutes that take people years to learn. Financial modelling. Marketing strategy. Operational systems. But those ten minutes sit on top of hundreds of hours of figuring out why it kept going wrong. The attention to detail is the whole game.

That is what people miss. The ten minutes look effortless from the outside. It is not. There is an entire iceberg underneath, and most people are standing on the tip arguing about whether the water is cold.

AI is not a shortcut. It is a skill. And right now, almost nobody is treating it like one.
