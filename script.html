Script

12 October 2025 · 1 min read

Study hard, get into a good college, become an engineer or a doctor, find a stable job. Everyone around you is following it. Your relatives measure you by it. Your school prepares you for it. And for a long time, questioning it feels almost disrespectful.

I questioned it anyway.

My family was always clear. Study, then you will be something in life. And I am not saying they were wrong. But I do not think they were fully right either. Because if you put a scientist on a football field and tell him to perform, he will struggle. Not because he is not smart or hardworking, but because that is simply not where he belongs.

For most of my school life, I was that scientist on the wrong field. I attend an IB school, get average grades, and used to believe I was going to be a basketball player. I represented India at the United World Games in Austria. I competed in ISSO, TAISI, and tournaments across the country. Basketball was my whole plan.

Then after tenth grade, something shifted. I started using AI, first for homework, then presentations, then studying and making notes, slowly going deeper, then learning to build websites, then apps, then actual businesses. I know I am not done. It is a long ride. But for the first time, I felt like I was in the right field.

That is the thing nobody puts in the script. Finding what you are good at is not a distraction from success. It is the whole point.

Every student deserves to find their field. Every parent deserves to know that their child finding it is not failure. It is the best possible outcome.

I am not anti-college. I am anti-autopilot.
